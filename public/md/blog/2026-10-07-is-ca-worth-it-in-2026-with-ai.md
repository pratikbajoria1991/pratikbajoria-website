Is CA worth it in 2026 now that AI can do routine accounting? A practising CA weighs pass rates, membership growth and AI’s impact, and explains which skills make the qualification pay off.

Key takeaways

- Yes, if you treat CA as a qualification in judgement, accountability and the right to sign; it is a weak bet if you want routine accounting work.
- The exam is still hard: 14.07% of candidates who sat both groups passed CA Final in May 2026 (3,345 of 23,776).
- ICAI membership was 4,23,105 on 1 April 2025; most members work in industry, finance and other roles rather than practice.
- Articleship is two years under the new scheme, and that is where AI now changes the work most: less preparing, more reviewing.
- Skills that make CA pay off alongside AI: review and scepticism, data fluency, clear writing, controls thinking and domain depth. ICAI’s own AI courses are a sensible place to start once qualified.

Yes, CA is still worth it in 2026, but for a different reason than it was ten years ago. The qualification is no longer valuable because you can do the accounting; AI can increasingly do much of that. It is valuable because a chartered accountant is a regulated person who can sign, take responsibility and explain what the numbers mean. If you go in expecting to be paid for routine work, you will be disappointed. If you go in to become the person who reviews, judges and advises, the case is strong.

I am a CA, ex-Big 4, a founder, and I now help firms implement AI. Students and parents ask me this more than any other question, so here is my honest answer with the numbers.

## The hard part has not changed: it is a difficult exam

In ICAI’s [CA Final May 2026 result](https://www.icai.org/post/prc-caresult-may2026), 14.07% of candidates who sat both groups passed (3,345 out of 23,776). The Group I pass rate was 12% and Group II was 20.49%. That difficulty is exactly why the qualification still carries weight. It is also why you should go in with a realistic plan for several attempts and articleship years, not a fixed timeline.

## The profession is growing, but practice is not the only path

ICAI’s [76th Annual Report](https://www.jurishour.in/columns/india-has-around-4-23-lakh-active-ca/) put membership at 4,23,105 on 1 April 2025, up from 4,03,617 a year earlier. About 1.61 lakh members were in full-time or part-time practice; the majority work in industry, finance, consulting and other roles. So “doing CA” today often means a finance career in a company, a GCC or a start-up rather than running a practice. Those are also the roles where AI is changing the daily work fastest.

## What AI changes for a CA career

The World Economic Forum’s [Future of Jobs Report 2025](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/2-jobs-outlook/) expects accounting, bookkeeping and payroll clerks to be among the fastest-declining roles to 2030, with AI and information processing as primary drivers. In India, Microsoft and LinkedIn’s [2024 Work Trend Index](https://news.microsoft.com/en-in/92-of-indian-knowledge-workers-use-ai-in-the-workplace-finds-microsoft-and-linkedin-2024-work-trend-index/) found 92% of knowledge workers already use AI at work. Put simply: the clerical layer of accounting is shrinking, and the people who remain are expected to use AI as a matter of course. The same WEF report expects 39% of workers’ core skills to change by 2030, with AI and big data the fastest-growing skills.

For a CA, that means:

- **Articleship will feel different.** Less vouching and typing, more checking machine output and dealing with exceptions. Firms will want article assistants who can review, not just prepare. I set out what that looks like on an actual file in [AI for audit working papers in India](https://pratikbajoria.com/blog/2026-09-30-ai-for-audit-working-papers-in-india-what-a-ca-should-automate-and-what-must-stay-human).
- **Entry-level value moves up.** A newly qualified CA who can only do what a tool does will struggle on salary. One who can explain a variance to a CFO or defend a position in front of a department will not.
- **The signature becomes more valuable, not less.** Somebody has to be accountable for AI-assisted numbers. Regulators and clients want that to be a qualified person.

The institute itself is adapting: ICAI says it has [trained over 50,000 members in AI](https://www.icai.org/post/icai-ais2026-26062026) and launched an advanced AI certification for members in June 2026. See my note on [AI courses for chartered accountants in India](https://pratikbajoria.com/blog/2026-10-07-ai-courses-for-chartered-accountants-in-india-what-is-worth-it).

## What AI does, and what a CA is still paid for

The clearest way I can put it: AI is absorbing preparation, and the market is paying for review, judgement and accountability. Here is how that splits across the work a CA actually does.

How AI changes common CA tasks (my assessment from implementation work, not survey data)

| Task | What AI does well now | What stays with the CA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data entry, vouching, bank and ledger work | Reads documents, drafts entries, matches transactions, flags exceptions | Sampling, challenge of unusual items, sign-off |
| GST and TDS reconciliations | Cleans exports, matches invoices and credits, lists differences | Eligibility, rejections, the figure claimed, client advice |
| Notice replies and research | Summarises the notice, drafts a first reply, finds relevant provisions | The position taken, accuracy of every citation, what is filed |
| Audit working papers | Prepares lead schedules, analytics and first-draft commentary | Risk assessment, evidence evaluation, the opinion and signature |
| Management reporting and MIS | Builds standard tables and charts, drafts variance notes | The commentary the client acts on, and the recommendation |
| Client relationship | Drafts emails and meeting notes | Trust, negotiation and explaining what the numbers mean |

India’s audit regulator says the same thing in formal terms. NFRA’s July 2026 paper on technology in audit states that responsibility for the audit opinion remains with the auditor however sophisticated the tools, and that technology cannot be a substitute for professional judgement. That is the core of why the qualification keeps its value.

## Who should do CA in 2026

**It suits you if** you enjoy reasoning about why a number is what it is, you are willing to put in years of exam preparation, and you want a regulated qualification that lets you sign, advise and move between practice and industry.

**Think twice if** you mainly want a stable job doing defined, repetitive accounting tasks. That is exactly the work AI is absorbing, and you can reach it faster through other routes.

### A decision checklist

Answer these honestly. If most are “yes”, CA in 2026 is a strong choice. If most are “no”, look hard at alternatives before committing several years.

1. Do I enjoy working out *why* a number is what it is, not just producing it?
2. Can I commit to several years of study and two years of practical training, and to more than one attempt if needed?
3. Am I comfortable being accountable for a conclusion, and saying no to a client or employer when the numbers do not support them?
4. Will I learn to use AI tools well, and to check them, rather than avoid them?
5. Is there a domain I could see myself going deep in, such as GST, direct tax, audit or a sector?
6. Would I be happy in industry, a GCC or a start-up, not only in practice?
7. Am I choosing CA for the work it leads to, not only for the title?

## Skills that make CA pay off alongside AI

1. **Review and scepticism:** catching the confident wrong answer, whether from a junior or a model.
2. **Data fluency:** Excel done properly, a basic grasp of how data moves between Tally, GST and banks, and comfort with AI assistants. My guide to [AI tools for chartered accountants](https://pratikbajoria.com/blog/best-ai-tools-chartered-accountants-finance-professionals) is a starting point.
3. **Clear writing and explanation:** clients pay for clarity.
4. **Controls thinking:** knowing what data may go where, and why. The [AI controls checklist for CA firms](https://pratikbajoria.com/blog/ai-controls-checklist-ca-firms-india) shows how a firm should think about it.
5. **Domain depth:** GST, direct tax, audit or a sector. AI is broad; clients pay for depth.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is CA worth it in 2026?

Yes, if you want a regulated qualification built on judgement, accountability and the right to sign. The exam remains hard (14.07% of candidates who sat both groups passed CA Final in May 2026), and membership keeps growing. It is a weaker bet if you mainly want routine accounting work, which AI is absorbing.

### Will AI take over Chartered Accountant jobs?

AI is taking over routine tasks such as data entry, reconciliations and first drafts. It is not taking over signing, opinions, advice or accountability, which stay with qualified CAs. NFRA’s July 2026 paper confirms that responsibility for an audit opinion remains with the auditor whatever tools are used.

### How many chartered accountants are there in India?

ICAI’s 76th Annual Report put total membership at 4,23,105 on 1 April 2025, up from 4,03,617 a year earlier. About 1.61 lakh members were in full-time or part-time practice; the rest work in industry, finance and other roles.

### What was the CA Final pass rate in May 2026?

14.07% of candidates who appeared in both groups passed (3,345 of 23,776). Group I was 12% and Group II was 20.49%, according to ICAI’s result press release of 18 June 2026.

### How long is articleship under the new scheme?

Practical training is 2 years under ICAI’s New Scheme of Education and Training, according to ICAI’s FAQs on the scheme.

### Does ICAI offer AI training for CAs?

Yes. AICA Level 1 is a 3-day course with 18 structured CPE hours at ₹5,000 plus GST; Level 2 is a 5-day course with 30 CPE hours at ₹10,000 plus GST for Level 1 holders; Level 3 launched in June 2026. Students can use ICAI’s CA GPT.

### What skills should CA students learn for an AI future?

Review and professional scepticism, data and Excel fluency, comfort with AI assistants, clear writing, controls thinking and depth in a domain such as GST, direct tax or audit. The WEF expects AI and big data to be the fastest-growing skills to 2030.
