Advance Tax by 15 December 2026: How a CA Firm Can Use AI Without Getting the Estimate Wrong

How a CA firm can use AI for the 15 December 2026 advance tax instalment under the Income-tax Act, 2025: worksheets, AIS checks and client notes, with the estimate kept human.

Key takeaways

  • The third advance tax instalment for tax year 2026-27 is due on Tuesday, 15 December 2026, under the Income-tax Act, 2025.
  • By then at least 75% of the year’s estimated tax, after TDS and TCS, should be paid (section 408); presumptive-income clients pay by 15 March.
  • A December shortfall attracts 3% interest under section 425, measured against the income finally returned, with no safe margin.
  • Let AI build worksheets from books and the AIS, flag new income and draft client notes.
  • Keep the estimate, the rate table and the challan details with named people, and pay under the 2025 Act for tax year 2026-27.

The third advance tax instalment for 2026-27 is due on Tuesday, 15 December 2026. For most practices it is the busiest advance tax date of the year, and the one with the least room for error. It is also the first instalment under the Income-tax Act, 2025 where a client’s estimate is tested without any cushion.

December is when firms reach for AI in a hurry: a chatbot to “work out advance tax” from a ledger export, or to write a hundred reminders. One of those can cost a client interest if nobody checks it. Here is how I would split the work.

What the law asks for this December

Advance tax for tax year 2026-27 (financial year 2026-27) runs under the Income-tax Act, 2025, and the e-filing portal asks you to choose that Act when you pay, according to the Income Tax Department’s payment FAQs. Self-assessment tax for AY 2026-27 still goes under the 1961 Act, so the challan screen is itself a place where a rushed junior can go wrong. The rules that matter, as enacted in the Gazette text of the Act:

  • Who pays. Anyone whose tax for the year, after TDS and TCS, is ₹10,000 or more (sections 404 and 405). A resident individual aged 60 or over with no business or professional income is outside the scheme (section 403(3)).
  • How much by 15 December. At least 75% of the estimated tax for the year, cumulative, less what was paid in June and September (section 408). Presumptive-income clients under section 58(2) pay the whole amount by 15 March instead.
  • What a shortfall costs. Under section 425, interest of 3% on the shortfall against 75% of the tax due on the income finally returned. June and September have a margin (no interest if 12% and 36% are paid); December does not. Section 424 separately charges 1% a month if total advance tax falls below 90% of the assessed tax.
  • Room to correct. A client can raise or lower later instalments as the estimate changes (section 406(2)), and anything paid by 31 March counts as advance tax for the year (section 408(3)).

The December test is measured against income the client eventually returns, not what anyone guessed in November. A poor estimate is not neutral.

A quick illustration, with made-up numbers: a client’s tax for the year after TDS turns out to be ₹4,00,000. By 15 December they should have paid ₹3,00,000. If they paid ₹1,80,000 in September and nothing more, the December shortfall is ₹1,20,000 and section 425 interest on it is ₹3,600. Small, but multiply it across a client list.

Where AI genuinely helps

Advance tax work is mostly collection and arithmetic around a few judgement calls. AI earns its place in the collection and the arithmetic.

  1. Gathering the numbers. For each client you need books to the latest close, TDS and TCS so far, and what has already been paid. The Annual Information Statement shows TDS and TCS, reported transactions such as dividends, interest and securities sales, and taxes already paid, including advance tax. AI can turn a client’s AIS download and a trial balance export into one standard worksheet, if the files come in through a controlled route.
  2. Projecting the year. Once the eight months to November are in, a model can draft a projection: run-rate for regular income, known one-offs listed separately, and last year’s pattern as a sense check. The draft is a starting point for the partner’s call, not the estimate.
  3. Spotting what changed. This is where it is most useful. Ask it to compare the AIS against the client’s own records and list anything new: a property sale, a large dividend, interest from a fresh deposit, an ESOP sale. Those are the items that blow up an estimate, and section 425(4) gives relief for some of them, such as capital gains and dividends, only if the tax is paid in a later instalment once the income arises.
  4. Client communication. Drafting the reminder, the computation note and the “here is why your instalment went up” email is low risk and high volume. My pricing post covers how that saved time should show up in billing.

For the books side, the same discipline as my Tally Prime guide and the bank statement categorisation workflow applies: an estimate built on uncategorised entries is a guess with decimals.

Where it must not decide

Three things stay with a named person.

The estimate itself. A model extends a trend with confidence. It does not know that a large customer has stopped ordering or that a property sale is planned for February. The partner, or the senior who knows the client, signs the number.

Rates and regime. Slab rates, surcharge, the regime the client has chosen and any deductions come from the Finance Act for the year and the client’s own position. Do not let a general model supply rates from memory; it learnt mostly from earlier years and from the 1961 Act. I made the same argument about section numbers in the Income-tax Act, 2025 transition post. Put the rates in a reviewed table and have the tool read from it.

The challan. Which Act, which tax year, which type of payment and which PAN are checked by a human before anything is paid. A payment under the wrong head is fixable, but slow.

Client data, before anything is uploaded

An AIS is one of the most personal documents a client has: bank interest, investments, property and salary in a single file. It should go only into a tool the firm has approved, with retention and training settings checked, and only the fields the worksheet needs. My DPDP rubric for CA firms sets out how to decide. If your team already reconciles TDS with AI, reuse that pipeline; the TDS reconciliation guide covers the matching logic, and the credits it confirms feed straight into the advance tax worksheet.

Keep the December worksheet: it is the starting point for March and, later, for the return, as the ITR filing post explains.

Monday-morning checklist

  • Pull the list of clients who paid in June or September, or who should have; they are your December list.
  • Ask each client for the latest AIS and books to 30 November, through the firm’s approved upload route only.
  • Put this year’s rates, surcharge and regime choices in one reviewed table; ban rates typed from memory.
  • Run AI on the worksheet build and the “what changed in AIS” flags; route every flag to a senior.
  • Have the partner sign each estimate, and note the one-offs expected before 31 March.
  • Check every challan for Act (2025), tax year (2026-27), payment type and PAN before 15 December.

Frequently asked questions

What is the advance tax due date in December 2026?

15 December 2026, a Tuesday. By then cumulative advance tax for tax year 2026-27 should be at least 75% of the estimated tax for the year, less TDS and TCS, under section 408 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Presumptive-income taxpayers under section 58(2) pay the whole amount by 15 March 2027.

What interest applies if the December instalment is short?

Under section 425, interest of 3% on the shortfall against 75% of the tax due on the income finally returned. Unlike June and September, there is no lower safe threshold in December. Section 424 separately charges 1% a month if advance tax paid for the year is below 90% of the assessed tax.

Can AI calculate advance tax for a CA firm’s clients?

It can build the worksheet, project the year and flag new income in the AIS, and it can calculate from a rate table your firm has reviewed. The estimate, the rates and the challan should be checked and signed by a named person, because interest is charged on the income finally returned.
