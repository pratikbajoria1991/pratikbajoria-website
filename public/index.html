From ambiguity
to shipped.

A deliberately unglamorous four-stage engagement. No pilots that never leave the lab, no decks that gather dust.

STEP 01

Diagnose

Two weeks inside your operations. I map workflows, interview your team and quantify where time and margin are leaking.

STEP 02

Prioritise

Every candidate use case scored on business impact, implementation effort and risk. You get a ranked roadmap, not a wish list.

STEP 03

Build

We ship the highest-value use case first, in weeks rather than quarters. Real system, real users, real measurement.

STEP 04

Embed

Training, documentation and handover so the capability outlives the engagement. Optional retainer for the next horizon.

About

Chartered Accountant.
AI practitioner.

I’m Pratik Bajoria — a Chartered Accountant with over nine years of post-qualification experience across audit, financial analysis, taxation and business process transformation. Part of that career was spent at a Big-4 firm; the rest across industry roles and, latterly, building my own ventures.

The Big-4 years taught me something most AI consultants never learn: how enterprise processes actually behave under pressure. Materiality thresholds. Control environments. Why a workflow that looks inefficient on paper often exists for a very good reason. The years since — in operating roles and as a founder — taught me the other half: how to actually ship something inside a business that has limited budget and no patience for theory.

My advantage isn’t that I understand AI. Plenty of people do. It’s that I spent nearly a decade inside the finance and operations functions AI is meant to transform, so I can tell a genuine opportunity from an expensive distraction before you commit budget to it.

Today I split my time between advising companies on AI implementation and building Findost, my AI-powered wealth management platform. Everything I recommend to clients, I have shipped myself.

Most AI projects fail before a single line of code is written. Not because the technology is immature, but because the problem was never properly defined.

Start with work that is repetitive, judgement-light and measurable. If you cannot state what good looks like in numbers — hours saved, error rate reduced, cycle time shortened — you are not ready to automate it.

Scope narrowly and ship fast. One workflow, one team, one metric is how you build the internal credibility that funds everything after it.
